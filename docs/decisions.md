# Decisions (these override docs/mvp-plan.md where they conflict)

1. Repo: single Next.js app with folders mirroring spec section 42 (workers/, data/, docs/ at the root; shared code under src/). Not a monorepo.
2. Admin: route group inside the web app, behind authentication on every page and API route.
3. PostGIS: yes, from the start.
4. Reset statuses: keep all 8 from spec section 4, plus the dimension fields.
5. License: [MIT / Apache 2.0]
6. Review policy: all AI-extracted events require human review at launch. Log every
   approve/edit/reject with source and event type so accuracy can be measured. Revisit
   auto-publishing for structured official sources once there is data.
7. Taxonomy: 24 types as listed in spec section 25.
8. Workers language: TypeScript, not Python (overrides spec section 43). Workers live under workers/ and run with tsx, sharing Node, pg, and tooling with the web app. Decided at step 2.1; Python was not installed and one toolchain is simpler to maintain.
9. No automated Telegram or Discord collection. Telegram's Content Licensing and AI Scraping terms and API terms §1.5 prohibit automated aggregation and AI use of platform data; Discord forbids self-bots and only allows bots invited by a server's admins. No Telegram migration, collector, or manual lead form is to be built.
10. Tier 4 publishing rule (for the review/approve step; to implement later): an event whose only SUPPORTS sources are Tier 4 cannot be published. It needs at least one Tier 1-3 supporting source.
11. Positions from Tier 4 sources: when a Tier 4 source reports a precise or current position, set location precision to District or Region, remove coordinates, and hold publication for 72 hours. Positions reported by official sources or established media can be shown as the source reported them.
12. Contradiction detection (MVP step 2.4) stays a reviewer action at launch: the reviewer sets `contradiction_flag`, its notes, and CONTRADICTS sources. Revisit when volume exceeds about 20 approvals a day, or after a contradiction is missed.
13. Confidence assessment (MVP step 2.5) is covered by the reviewer's choice at approval. The approve form shows a rule-based suggestion from the attached sources' tiers and types (no AI call); the reviewer decides.
14. Map dots (amends spec section 60 for the regional map only; requested and approved by the project owner). The map may draw one dot per area (a country, Kaliningrad Oblast, western Russia, the Baltic Sea, the Gulf of Finland) or per admin-1 region, at a fixed, hand-set anchor stored in data/map-anchors.json. Anchors are chosen once, in open land or sea away from towns and known military sites, rounded to 0.1 degrees, and never derived from, moved by, or jittered for events. Only the dot's size and colour change, in three count steps on an amber-to-orange heat scale (src/lib/map-style.ts, HEAT_SCALE; no red). The scale shows reporting volume, not intensity of activity, and the legend keeps the notes "Each dot marks an area, not a location." and "Counts reflect reporting, not intensity of activity." A dot marks an area, not a location. No per-event markers, no centroids of events, and no event coordinates reach the client. The rule against showing precise or current positions is unchanged, and the 72-hour exclusion of items supported only by Tier 4 sources still applies to the map. Items about places outside the theater are counted in cards beside the map, with no dots.
15. NATO content in the historical record (approved by the project owner, 2026-10-03). NATO's "Use of NATO content and brand" page (nato.int/en/about-us/official-texts-and-resources/use-of-nato-content-and-brand) allows credited reuse: "All content taken from NATO and republished must be clearly credited or sourced to NATO."; "Photos, videos and articles are released under the legally recognized terms of 'Fair Use' to members of the press, academia, non-profits and the general public."; "Material is provided, free of charge, for use in objective and balanced content, even if at times the end products may be critical of NATO."; "No material produced by NATO is to be sold, used for outside advertising or promotional purposes of any kind."; "NATO reserves the right to request the removal of NATO copyrighted material from any externally created content." The page is silent on automated access and AI use. Historical NATO articles are processed on the same basis as the live NATO feed, on these conditions: text articles only (no photos, video or multimedia); every NATO event credits NATO as its source; only a short excerpt and our own summary are stored; nothing is used for promotion or sale; and any NATO removal request is honoured.

22. Live statements (the project owner's decision, 2026-10-05). An event may cite a statement the reviewer saw live, before any registry source reports it, through the reserved registry source "Live statement (broadcast)" (Tier 1, OFFICIAL_GOVERNMENT; migration 0013). Each use names the speaker and where it was seen; the link is optional. It counts as a Tier 1 official source under decision 10, so it can support publishing alone. It is used only when the event reports the speaker's own words, and the registry source is attached once outlets publish the story. Confidence stays the reviewer's choice at approval.
23. Removing and merging published events (the project owner's decision, 2026-10-05). A published event can be removed from the site: it becomes REJECTED with a logged reason (review action REJECT, previous status PUBLISHED) and disappears from every public page, the map, the sitemap and open data. Nothing is deleted, so the append-only review log (decision 6) stays whole. A published event can be merged only into another published event; its sources move to the target, it becomes MERGED, and its public link permanently redirects to the target.
24. Digest sections and Historical Context (the project owner's decision, 2026-10-06; overrides the earlier rule that the digest's Historical Context is written by code and states counts only). Every digest section is a short summary: topical sections one or two sentences and at most 50 words, Contradictions & Unverified Reporting at most three sentences and 70 words, the Summary unchanged (two sentences, 45 words); checked in code. Historical Context is written by the model from up to 20 published historical events of the day's types or countries: two to four sentences, at most 90 words, interpreting how the day's activity compares with the historical record. Interpretation and opinion are allowed in that section only, and it is labelled on the digest page as analysis, not a finding. Predictive language stays banned everywhere, and each sentence must cite at least one of the day's events. When there is nothing to compare, the fixed line is used.

26. Exercise-type events and exercise records (the project owner's decision, 2026-10-06). An event of type Exercise describes the exercise itself, so when it is published (at approval, or when a published one is saved) it is linked to the exercise of the same name, or, if there is none, a new exercise is created from it: name (the event's exercise name, or its headline), status, country, location, units, personnel, equipment, dates (the event date as the observed start once it has begun), summary, and its supporting sources that have a link. The new exercise is published when it has such a source and stays a draft otherwise; reset fields start as Unknown. Creating and linking are logged (exercise CREATE, event EDIT). A "Create exercise from this event" button on the review page does the same for events published earlier. Events of other types are never turned into exercises.
